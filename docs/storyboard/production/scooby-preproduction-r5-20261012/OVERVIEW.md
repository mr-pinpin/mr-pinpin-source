# Scooby Gets a Bath — R5 preproduction

Status: full revision in development after the user's quality reset. The published
Bath Magic R24 edition is the editorial and visual baseline. The live Scooby V3
and its PDFs remain unchanged until a complete replacement passes review. R4 was
not published; its smiling studies and unneeded staircase plan are superseded.

## Story spine

A familiar family morning opens the story. Papa takes Scooby for a short walk and
some practice while PinPin and Rabbit go to play with leaf boats. Scooby's nose
leads him back to the garden scrape from the previous chapter. A blue wooden edge
and a tiny bell lead to Papa's lost toy boat. The discovery brings both paths back
together. Digging and a muddy roll leave Scooby conspicuously dirty; a practiced
"wait" lets Papa and the children finish safely. There is no deep-pit rescue and
no staircase. At home, the adventure gives way to patient care: a reluctant dog,
a real wash and rinse, an enormous shake, drying and brushing. The children's
boat game receives a payoff and farewell. Clean Scooby returns to the house he
already belongs to, then rests beside his family. Sleep is the quiet end of an
active day, not a reward for becoming worthy of a home.

The object is a **small faded-blue wooden toy boat**, cream rim, short mast stump,
and one tiny brass bell tied at its bow. Papa mentions the missing boat before
the outing, connecting it to the children's leaf-boat memory from Bath Magic.
The blue edge and sound are revealed before the hull. Its discovery earns
recognition and a simple boat-game payoff; it is never an unexplained full-size
rowing boat or a magic artifact. The mast remains a stump throughout this day.

## Six acts and pace

1. **Morning and practice, scenes01–10.** Breakfast has real pauses and gestures;
   Papa's lost boat is planted in conversation. Cleanup and ball play precede the
   outing. Papa takes Scooby alone for practice. PinPin has not teleported into
   their walk.
2. **Paths separate, scenes11–17.** Rabbit arrives, Mama agrees to a short outing,
   and a wide fork establishes the garden path versus the spring path. Papa
   checks the rescued plant; children play with two leaves. The spade's position
   is established before use.
3. **Scent and discovery, scenes18–25.** Scratch, blue edge, sound, return of the
   children, partial exposure, full recognition. Dirt grows visibly and remains
   through every later pre-wash frame. Scooby takes an ordinary step out of a
   shallow scrape. No stuck dog, invented stairs or unrelated civil-engineering
   subplot. Papa's wait cue has a consequence.
4. **Home and consent, scenes26–35.** Carry the three boats correctly. Mama sees
   the mud. Objects are put aside; the empty portable bath is brought over and warm water is carried/poured before it is checked. Scooby watches, sniffs,
   hesitates, then places one paw and eventually the others in the bath.
5. **Washing and shake, scenes36–45.** Water, shampoo/brush, individual paw cleaning,
   complete rinse, towel, pause, shake, reaction, drying and loose-coat brushing.
   Every operation has a visible cause and changed state. Expressions follow
   what happens; the surprise is not played as uninterrupted grinning.
6. **Payoffs and rest, scenes46–52.** Children clean the boat and try three boats;
   Rabbit leaves with his two leaves. The corrected doorway shows whole bodies
   at the previous chapter's scale. Papa tells the short boat memory; yawns and
   a quiet cushion close the day. Mama/PomPom's care continues.

Fifty-two narrative illustrations plus one localized title page: 53 reader/PDF
pages. The count follows these beats. Each caption is one small action or turn,
with space for the art to act. A page does not introduce another arbitrary event.

## Visual continuity and acting contract

- Published Bath Magic R17/R18/R24 room, doorway, garden, cast and spring images
  are the stated visual references. Their house has one central solid green
  arched front door between two round radial windows, warm wood floor, round
  table, stove and cabinets. The separate measured three-room model remains
  unchanged; its R4 renders are recorded studies, not approved reference-led
  illustration geometry. No measured-conformance claim is made for image-led art.
- Papa: broad adult, short rounded muzzle, cool grey-tipped quills and expressive
  visible eyes. Mama: adult russet quills and cream face. PinPin is the older child;
  PomPom a distinct supported infant. Mama has two arms. Rabbit uses the canonical
  grey-beige/pink-ear design. The two birds stay the same established pair.
- Scooby uses the published brown/golden mount-sized dog identity, cream chest,
  floppy ears and curved tail. Withers are near Papa's standing head on common
  ground; dog head is higher. Use the prior chapter's coat and face, not a new
  fluffy toy-dog redesign. Paw width is small relative to Papa's torso. No huge
  foreground paw standing in for the dog at the door.
- A style reference supplies identity, **not a frozen smile**. Every visible actor
  receives eyes, brow, mouth, eyeline and posture direction. Default mouths are
  relaxed or quietly speaking. Brief delight is earned at bread, baby play,
  recognition or payoff. Papa can concentrate, be firm, look wry or be surprised;
  Mama can raise one eyebrow; children can be puzzled, attentive or startled;
  Scooby can sniff with mouth closed, perk ears, doubt, relax and yawn.
- Each location gets an establishing image and a stable visual signature. The
  garden fence/tree, path, plant, flat stone, spade and house stay fixed throughout
  its sequence. Closer shots inherit the preceding selected image; a changed
  viewpoint must be explicitly planned, with no unexplained scene reversal.
- Follow `OBJECTS.json` and per-scene states in `PLAN.json`. Hidden objects are
  occluded or offscreen, never duplicated. Baby support and hand occupations must
  be physically possible. A boat/jug/spade cannot occupy the same hand at once.
- Mud begins on forepaws, spreads to all legs/belly/chest/flank, and covers a large
  visible portion of the lower body after rolling. It does not reset before wash.
  First immersion does not clean the rest of the dog. Suds and rinsing remove dirt
  progressively; all paws and coat are clean only after the final rinse.
- Exact prompts, input hashes, native masters and per-image visible review remain
  separate from selection claims. Review in sequence as well as alone: cast,
  scale, object shapes/counts/positions, dirt/water and actual acting. No image
  passes because the file exists or the model was told the correct words.
