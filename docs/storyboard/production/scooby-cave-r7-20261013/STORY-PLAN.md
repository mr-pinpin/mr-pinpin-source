# Discovery with evidence, choices and consequences

Proposed direction, awaiting the author's identity decision. Preserve the
family-care/bath story and the prior books' rules. PinPin is a curious child,
Papa is a practical parent, Mama is an active family member, PomPom a supported
infant, Rabbit a thinking partner and Scooby a large useful dog. The Elder's
existing kindness is not retrospectively declared deception.

## The local dramatic question

**Who has tended the water below the forest, and why is the surface receiving
so little of it?** The children first seek a place, then understand a system,
then must decide how to help without damaging what they discovered.

Veles is the proposed maker/caretaker. His possible motive is to keep an
underground refuge alive through dry seasons. The children's motive is to
restore enough flow for their surface neighbours. Those aims can conflict over
how much water the outlet releases, without making either party generically evil.
Nature supplies the rock; whether Veles excavated whole chambers or modified
channels remains an explicit author choice, not an unsupported reveal.

This chapter establishes an earned connection to Veles. A later storm story can
develop Perun. Do not insert a cosmic war, amnesia twist, instant lightning
vision, talking exposition dump or retroactive stolen-cloud explanation here.

## Evidence ladder — no conclusion from one attractive clue

1. **Cool draft + damp blossom:** an opening exists; no ownership conclusion.
2. **Growing light beyond the entrance:** a sustained habitat; distinguish roots,
   skylight and luminous vines instead of giving every chamber identical light.
3. **Repeated trained vines + braided channel:** evidence of tending, not proof of
   a god. Rabbit offers a reasonable alternative: another forest neighbour.
4. **Returning leaf + hollow echo:** something larger is concealed by the bend.
5. **Lake:** confirms stored water, not theft. Quiet awe comes before dialogue.
6. **Worked root gate + water marks:** someone has controlled a release over time.
7. **Caretaker trace + informed recognition:** grounds the proposed Veles link.
   The exact trace and recognition need a design sheet before finished art.
8. **Measured small release + changed surface flow:** demonstrates the system
   rather than claiming the child saved everything through unexplained new magic.

## Roles and subplots

- **Scooby:** useful nose, recall of the old dig, obedience that permits a safe
  pause, persistent effort at the clay outlet, reluctant wash, deserved rest.
- **PinPin/Rabbit:** different guesses, a failed prediction, a test with one leaf,
  changed map, disagreement over opening the gate, a shared successful experiment.
- **Papa:** knows footing/loads rather than all magical answers; pauses the
  overlarge release, asks the children to observe and later welcomes the dog.
- **Mama/PomPom:** food/warmth, supported sensory attention, echo call/answer,
  dry-bank base for the observation. Mama notices the waterline change while the
  scouts stare at the gate: she contributes evidence, not only reassurance.
- **Elder:** responds to observations instead of solving the mystery on arrival.
  His connection to Veles remains pending; no deception is inferred from kindness.
- **Beaver:** verifies the outlet's effect from his established bank and explains
  a practical limit. His intervention pays off the surface-water question.

## Ending and continuing mystery

Resolve the small problem completely: a stable modest outlet supplies the
surface without draining the underground garden. The children can show why.
The bath/rest resolution completes Scooby's daily arc. A final caretaker clue
opens a future chapter about Veles, but does not substitute for this chapter's
payoff. Retain the lake on the map, show the marked tested gate state, and return
home by a mapped route; do not make the cavern disappear after its reveal.

## Family movement and nursery levels

Mama/PomPom initially wait at R1. Papa explicitly checks the broad approach,
returns and escorts them to the dry shore station before Mama observes the
lake waterline; they never teleport from the porch to the gate. No baby walks
or approaches the lake unsupported. The keeper alcove dry bank is at−0.5; its
inset nursery basins have beds below−0.8 and share the lake water level. Lake
water does not flow uphill onto the dry bank. An excessive gate opening floods
the LOWER outlet bank downstream while lowering the lake and drying its shallows.
